# 🎥 Demo Video Script — Build Stage (3:00)

**Format:** one recording, **3:00 total**.
**Why 3:00:** Guidelines §4, Stage 2 requires *"a 3-minute demo video … uploaded on YouTube"*, and *"judges are not required to watch the video beyond the first three minutes."*

**Structure:** the first **2:00** stands alone as the pitch (and is the version to reuse for mentor calls). The final **60s** is the Build-stage evidence block — cut it if you need a pure 2:00 pitch.

> ⚠️ **No fabricated data.** Every number spoken on camera must come from a logged measurement (`INTERVIEW_KIT.md`) or a public source (ASER 2024 / UDISE+ 2024-25 / NIPUN Bharat). Placeholders are marked `[FROM LOG]` — fill them from real records or say "we haven't measured that yet."

**Production rules**
- Record on a **real low-end Android device** (Android 8-class, 1–2 GB RAM). Never a flagship, never an emulator — the rubric grades low-cost device behaviour.
- Put the device in **airplane mode for the whole shoot**, and show the toggle on camera at 1:40.
- One continuous take for the loop (0:15–1:40) if possible. Cutaways are fine; faked state changes are not.
- Burn in **English subtitles**; keep the on-screen UI in **Hindi**. Show the vernacular interface, don't describe it.
- Avoid result-animation flourishes. The credibility of this video is its plainness.

## 2:00 PITCH VERSION

### 0:00–0:15 · Hook — one room, three grades
**Visual:** wide shot of an actual multigrade classroom (with permission, no child faces in close-up). One teacher, children of visibly different ages, one blackboard.
**On-screen:** *Two-thirds of India's Grade 1–2 classrooms have more than one grade in them. (UDISE+ 2024-25)*
**VO:** "This is one classroom. Three grades. One teacher. Every teaching material in the country assumes one grade, one level — and this room is none of those things."

### 0:15–0:35 · The morning check
**Visual:** Screen recording. Teacher opens the app → picks a child → child reads aloud → waveform → transcript and confidence appear. **Airplane-mode icon visible.**
**On-screen:** *3 minutes · voice · offline*
**VO:** "So we start with three minutes. A child reads aloud, on the device, with no internet. The app hears the reading and places the child on a level — this is a sensor, nothing more."

### 0:35–0:50 · The room map
**Visual:** The room map fills in: desks lighting up by band; "help first" chips appear.
**On-screen:** *Who can actually do what — not who is in which grade*
**VO:** "Thirty-four children, and now the teacher can see where every one of them actually is. Not their grade. Their level."

### 0:50–1:15 · The plan ← **the hero moment**
**Visual:** Teacher taps one chapter. Loading. A 40-minute card appears: opening, three station cards, two rotations, Hindi talk-lines, a timer.
**On-screen:** *One chapter → three levels → one 40-minute block*
**VO:** "Then the part that has never existed. She picks today's chapter, and the app builds her forty minutes: a shared opening, three stations at three different levels — every activity tied to a page in the book she's already holding — and a line telling her exactly what to say at each one."

### 1:15–1:40 · Running it
**Visual:** Teacher walks to Band A, reads the talk-line aloud; timer running; taps "help first" child; corrects a level with one tap.
**On-screen:** *Weakest band first · teacher can always override*
**VO:** "She starts with the weakest group, because the app knows they need her most. And when it gets a child wrong — it will — one tap fixes it. The teacher always outranks the model."

### 1:40–1:55 · Offline proof + guardrails
**Visual:** Toggle airplane mode ON on camera → run the whole loop again → it works. Then show a rejected, uncited AI output failing the validator.
**On-screen:** *Airplane mode · works. Every activity must cite an NCERT page.*
**VO:** "No network, and nothing stops — assessing, grouping and running a plan are all local. When the network does come back, the AI can write new plans, but anything that doesn't cite a real page in a real textbook gets thrown away before she sees it."

### 1:55–2:00 · Close
**Visual:** Card: product name, track, QR key visual.
**On-screen:** *Samanantar - Challenge 02: Learning-Level Visibility · ₹0 per child · offline-first*
**VO:** "Same textbooks. Same phone. Same teacher. Samanantar."

## +60s BUILD-STAGE EVIDENCE EXTENSION (2:00 → 3:00)

This block exists because the Build stage scores **user-centricity and proof of testing at 35%** and **technical strength at 30%** — and because saying what you *couldn't* measure is what makes the rest believable.

### 2:00–2:25 · What teachers told us
**Visual:** Clips or photos from real interviews (adult consent on file, no child footage). On-screen quote cards with teacher IDs.
**On-screen:** *T-01 · [date] · [district withheld]*
**VO:** "We sat with [N] teachers before we built anything. Two things changed the product." *(use a real finding, e.g. the time lost per period, or that the 40-minute unit was wrong and the teacher asked for 25)*
**Rule:** if a quote isn't in the evidence log with a date and ID, it does not go in the video.

### 2:25–2:45 · What we measured
**Visual:** The stopwatch baseline sheet; the task-success table from the usability test.
**On-screen:** *Paper assessment: [FROM LOG] min/child → with Samanantar: [FROM LOG] min/child*
**VO:** "On paper, assessing one child took her [FROM LOG]. With the app, [FROM LOG]. [X] of 5 tasks completed unaided by [Y] of 3 teachers."
**Rule:** no comparison without both numbers from the log.

### 2:45–3:00 · What we changed, and what we don't know
**Visual:** The change log (`INTERVIEW_KIT.md` §7) scrolling, then a plain card.
**On-screen:** *Three weeks cannot prove learning gains. Here's what we'll measure next.*
**VO:** "Three weeks cannot show learning gains, so we don't claim any. What we have is teacher time, plan completion and grouping fidelity — and a validated design. The learning-gain study is the next stage."
