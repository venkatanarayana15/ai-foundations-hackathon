AI for Foundational Learning Hackathon
Guidelines
OBJECTIVE-
The hackathon aims to surface breakthrough novel ideas and prototypes, that go beyond
existing approaches, to key foundational learning challenges, leveraging the power of
frontier AI to develop solutions with the potential for meaningful impact.

===== PAGE 2 =====
SECTION 1 - HACKATHON ROADMAP
For schedule and timelines please refer to the hackathon landing page
SECTION 2 - ELIGIBILITY AND REGISTRATION
All participants must register individually to participate in the hackathon.
1. Participants: Hackathon is open to students, tech and AI enthusiasts, educators,
professionals working in not-for-profits, innovators and problem solvers.
2. Age Eligibility: The hackathon is open to individuals who are 18 years of age or older
at the time of registration.

===== PAGE 3 =====
3. Citizenship Eligibility: The Hackathon is open to Indian citizens, including Indian
students and professionals who are currently studying or working abroad.
4. Individual Registration: Registration is open to individual participants and not to
organizations. Participants may be affiliated with academic institutions, NGO,
company, or other organization; however, they must register and participate in their
individual capacity and not on behalf of or as a representative of that organization
5. The Hackathon is not open to employees of Central Square Foundation given its
role in organizing and executing this Hackathon
SECTION 3 — PARTICIPATION RULES
1. Team Size: Teams are expected to have 2–4 members.
2. One Team per Participant: Each participant may be a member of only one team.
3. Multidisciplinary Teams: Multidisciplinary teams are encouraged. Having at least
one education domain expert and one AI/ML domain expert on each team is highly
recommended to bring diverse expertise and perspectives to the solution, but is not
mandatory.
4. Submission for multiple challenges: Each team may make more than one
submission. However, each idea submission should be unique and substantially
different. Further, once a team’s submission advances to a subsequent stage, the
team must continue working on submission for which it was selected.
5. Changes after the ideate stage: No additions or substitutions to the team or
changes to the selected challenge(s) are allowed after the ideate stage.
6. Partial Solutions: Solutions do not need to address the entire challenge as
articulated. Teams may develop and submit a solution that addresses a specific
sub-part or aspect of the challenge.
For example for Challenge 4 (as shown below) teams can choose to focus only on
sub-part of the challenge as highlighted below, without addressing the other
sub-points

===== PAGE 4 =====
7. AI-Centric Solutions: AI must be central to the solution developed, and should
leverage (as best possible) the advanced capabilities that frontier LLM models and
AI tools offer. Source-code shared as part of the Build stage submission will be
used to verify how AI is being used in the solution.
8. Judging Decisions: Decisions made by the judging panel at each stage of the
Hackathon are final.
SECTION 4 - SUBMISSIONS
Stage 1 — Ideate Stage
Format: Online
Submission deadline: 27 September 2026, 11:59pm
Teams must submit:
1. A proposal of 7–10 powerpoint slides responding to the selected Foundational
Learning challenge/ sub-part of the challenge. Please note that the jury will not
review any slides beyond the prescribed 10-slide limit. The page count does not
include the Title Page, Table of Contents, or Team Profiles. The proposal should
cover:
○ Problem Understanding (1 page): The specific aspect of the challenge the
team seeks to address, including who is affected and how.

===== PAGE 5 =====
○ Proposed Solution (1-2 pages): A description of the proposed AI-led solution,
how it addresses the chosen challenge and why it is expected to improve
outcomes
○ Users and Context (1-2 pages): Intended user, user journey map and any
planned features in consideration of existing user constraints
○ Innovation and creativity of idea (1-2 pages): The novel AI-centered
approach, capability, or application the solution adopts when addressing the
challenge, and how it compares against existing competition solutions to
make it substantially better and differentiated.
○ Technology and Data feasibility plan (1-2 pages): Proposed tools, models,
and tech stack planned to build the solution, and include a simple data-flow
diagram showing how information moves through your solution, from user
input to output, and highlighting the role that AI would play in the solution.
Submission Format: The proposal must be submitted as a PDF through the
Hack2Skill platform.
Stage 2 — Build Stage
Format: Online
Submission deadline: 1st November, 2026, 11:59pm
Teams must submit:
● Demo Version: A link to a testable version of the application submitted, along with
any software required to test the application
● Source Code: Teams must provide access to the GitHub source code or code
repository so that judges can verify how AI is being used in the solution.
● A 3-minute demo video: Link to a publically accessible video uploaded on YouTube
narrating and demonstrating the
○ Challenge addressed and how the solution addresses it
○ Walk through of the user interphase of the solution
○ Key features of the solution; and how they address the selected challenge.
○ Any functionality or user interface features that facilitate ease of usage by
the intended users
Note: Judges are not required to watch the video beyond the first three minutes.
● Short PowerPoint Deck: Teams must submit a short PowerPoint presentation
covering:
○ Effective AI use & Technical strength
■ AI/LLM usage: Explain where AI/LLM calls are made in the solution
and what they actually do. Show us the thinking in layers — data
input, processing, guardrails, evaluation, output.

===== PAGE 6 =====
■ Guardrails: Briefly describe how your solution deals with wrong or
inappropriate AI outputs (e.g., verification, guardrails, human review)
■ Ability of solution to work in real scenarios: State how will the
solution work in low-connectivity, low-data environments and on
low-cost devices. If parts require connectivity, tell us which, and what
degrades gracefully offline.
○ User Inputs and Testing (1–2 pages): Any data or evidence showing how user
feedback or inputs have been collected and incorporated into the solution.
○ Future Roadmap (1-2 pages): Intended pathways to further development of
the product, and integration into existing systems.
We highly recommend that the teams submit their ideas and solutions a few hours prior to the
deadline to allow room for bypassing last minute technical issues.

○ Walk through of the user interphase of the solution
○ Key features of the solution; and how they address the selected challenge.

SECTION 5 — RESOURCES AND SUPPORT
Reading List
1. Each challenge is accompanied by 1–2 reading resources to help participants
develop a better understanding of the problem.
2. There is also an overall reading list which includes a compendium of AI-powered
EdTech solutions that currently exist, and reports on existing challenges in
foundational learning delivery.
3. These resources are intended as a starting point only. Participants are encouraged
to conduct their own research and explore additional sources when developing their
solutions.
Resources to Help Build Solutions
1. Participants are provided with links to select publicly available resources, including
relevant data sources, teaching and learning materials used in public schools, and
other resources that may support solution development.
2. These resources are indicative and not exhaustive. Participants are encouraged to
identify and use additional relevant resources as appropriate.
Claude Credits
1. Build Stage Credits: The 30 teams progressing to the Build Stage will receive USD
500 per team in Claude LLM credits. Details on how the credits will be provided will
be shared with selected teams at the beginning of the Build Stage.
2. Use of Claude Credits: Teams must use the Claude LLM credits provided to them for
at least one part of their solution. In addition, teams may also use any other tools,
technologies, or LLMs that are best suited to their solution.
3. Final Stage Credits: The 10 teams selected for the Final Stage will receive an
additional USD 500 per team in Claude LLM credits for further solution development
and refinement.

===== PAGE 8 =====
4. Other Software and Infrastructure: No additional laboratory facilities, software, or
other development infrastructure will be provided to participants. Teams are
responsible for arranging any additional tools or resources they require.
Mentorship Sessions
1. Mentorship Support: The 30 teams progressing to the Build Stage will have the
option to book two 40-minute virtual mentorship sessions with experts of their
choice from our mentor panel:
○ One session with an education mentor; and
○ One session with a technical mentor.
2. Booking Process: Details regarding available slots and the process for booking
mentorship sessions will be shared with selected teams at the beginning of the
Build Stage.

SECTION 7 — PLAGIARISM & ORIGINALITY POLICY
1. All submitted solutions must be original and built during the hackathon window.
Products built before the hackathon, or solutions already submitted to another
competition, are not eligible.
2. Using open-source libraries, public APIs, datasets, and AI coding assistants,
including LLMs, is expected and encouraged. The core idea and implementation
must be the team's own.
3. Organizers and judges may review shared code repositories, ask teams to explain
their build process live, and cross check submissions against public sources and
other entries.

===== PAGE 11 =====
4. Any submission found in violation is disqualified at any stage, including after being
shortlisted or announced as a winner, in which case any prize already awarded is
forfeited.
5. A team disqualified for a suspected violation may request a review by writing to
edtechinnovationhub@centralsquarefoundation.org, with supporting evidence.
SECTION 8 - OWNERSHIP OF SUBMISSION
● Participants retain full ownership of the intellectual property (IP) of the code and
working prototype, submitted as part of the Hackathon.
● The Hackathon is being conducted with the aim of identifying innovative solutions
that can help improve the delivery of foundational education to low-income
learners.
With this objective in mind, teams are strongly encouraged, but not required, to
open-source their code and share the repository link as part of their submission, so
that their innovations can be further developed, adapted, and potentially used to
benefit learners and educators.
Teams that choose to open source their code will be awarded an additional USD
250 worth of Claude credits, over and above those given at the build and final stage.
However, choosing to open-source a code, or not to do so, will have no impact on the
team’s eligibility, judging, scoring, selection, or final outcome in the Hackathon.
SECTION 9 : ORGANIZER SHOWCASE RIGHTS AND SHARING OF INFORMATION
1. By submitting a project, participants grant the Hackathon organizers a
non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual license to use submitted material for
judging and for non-commercial promotional and marketing, with credit to the
team.
2. Sharing of Information with not-for-profit partners: Organizers have the right to
share the name and contact details of teams with any education focussed
organizations that are keen to collaborate on further development of the solution.
